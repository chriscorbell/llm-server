# Inference engines for Qwen3.8-27B on one Arc Pro B70 (Sept 2026)

## Hardware and OS facts

- Arc Pro B70: BMG-G31, 32 Xe2 cores, 256 XMX engines, 32 GB GDDR6 on 256-bit, 608 GB/s, PCIe 5.0 x16, $949, launched 25 March 2026 ([TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/346948/intel-arc-pro-b70-pro-viz-gpu-tested-with-bmg-g31-die), [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/202603281052_Intel_Arc_Pro_B70_32GB_AI_Workstation_GPU_Launch)). Measured: 128 TFLOPS FP16 matmul, 443 GB/s effective bandwidth ([IDFS](https://idfs.ai/blog/six-days-with-the-intel-arc-pro-b70)).
- Ubuntu 26.04 + Linux 7.0 + Mesa 26.0.3 + Compute Runtime 26.14 runs four B70s without trouble ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/review/intel-arc-pro-b70-four)). Upstream vLLM XPU docs recommend Compute Runtime 26.18+ for vllm-xpu-kernels v0.1.10+ ([vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/getting_started/installation/gpu/#intel-xpu)). The `xe` driver, not i915, is required.
- Qwen3.8-27B (released 14 Aug 2026, Apache 2.0): 27B dense, hybrid Gated DeltaNet linear attention with one full-attention block per four sub-blocks, native vision encoder (image and video), 262k native context, built-in MTP head, thinking on by default. Arch name is `Qwen3_5ForConditionalGeneration`. Official BF16 and FP8 checkpoints ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B), [vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)).

The hybrid GDN architecture is the key constraint: every engine needs working Gated DeltaNet kernels on Xe2, and this is where most failures have been reported.

## Quantization on Xe2: what runs natively

- XMX on Xe2 supports FP16, BF16, INT8, INT4, INT2. FP8 is not an XMX DPAS type on Xe2; Intel's Xe3 docs still list FP8 as missing on XMX ([oneAPI XMX guide](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/oneapi/optimization-guide-gpu/2024-1/xmx.html), [HWCooling Xe3](https://www.hwcooling.net/en/xe3-whats-new-in-panther-lakes-gpu-architecture-analysis/)). INT8 has 2x the FP16 throughput (4096 vs 2048 ops/clk per engine).
- oneDNN says optimized fp8 exists for "Xe2 Graphics," but its 2026 release notes carry a known issue that "fp8 matmul primitive may sporadically produce incorrect results on Intel Arc B-series" ([oneDNN release notes](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/release-notes/onednn/2026.html)). vllm-xpu-kernels register `fp8_gemm` and `fp8_gemm_w8a16`; the 2026 Q1 roadmap lists "WoQ compressed tensors on BMG: Wint4A16/Wfp8A16" ([roadmap #141](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm-xpu-kernels/issues/141)). Treat FP8 on B70 as weight-only storage with FP16 compute, not native W8A8.
- Community consensus from the B70 cookbook: FP8 block quant "currently lacks an optimized XPU scaling kernel"; GPTQ-INT4 via `XPUwNa16LinearKernel` is "the demonstrated optimal fast path" because "Intel's XMX engines are integer-first" ([cookbook](https://github.com/SergiioB/intel-arc-pro-b70-inference-cookbook/blob/master/docs/qwen38-27/QWEN38-VLLM-XPU.md)). Bentech got Qwen3.5-27B FP8 running under llm-scaler at ~13 tok/s at 80k context, so FP8 works but slowly ([Bentech](https://bentech.substack.com/p/running-local-llms-on-intel-arc-pro)).

Quality data:
- Quesma (26 Aug 2026) benchmarked Qwen3.8-27B GGUF: BF16, Q8_0, Q4_K_M all scored ~95% GPQA Diamond, ~80% IFBench, ~74% Terminal-Bench 2.1; UD-Q2_K_XL dropped Terminal-Bench to ~68%; 1-bit collapsed ([Quesma](https://quesma.com/blog/qwen38-27b-quantizations-benchmarked/)).
- Kaitchup (May 2026) on Qwen3.6-27B: FP8 and Intel AutoRound INT4 hold up; NVFP4 with quantized linear-attention layers "consistently and significantly underperforms." Keeping GDN `in_proj_a`/`in_proj_b` in higher precision matters ([Kaitchup](https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/qwen36-27b-quantization-fp8-vs-int4)). GPTQ-Int4 quant users on B70 follow the same rule, plus keep the 15 MTP tensors in BF16.
- No direct FP8 vs AWQ vs Q6_K study for Qwen3.8-27B was found.

Practical ordering for this hardware: BF16 (too big, 55 GB) > FP8 weight-only (fits, slow, correctness caveat in oneDNN) > GGUF Q8_0 (29 GB, now fast on SYCL, fits with little KV room) > Q6_K/Q5_K_M > GPTQ/AutoRound INT4 G128 (fastest, measured benchmark parity at 4-bit).

Details per engine:

**1. Upstream vLLM XPU.** Measured on a single B70 with `SergiioB/Qwen3.8-27B-GPTQ-Int4-sym-G128-MTP-BF16` on `vllm/vllm-openai-xpu` (vLLM 0.27.2rc1, xpu-kernels 0.1.12.3): 32.9 t/s decode without spec, 83.7 t/s with MTP4 (95% acceptance), 56.3 t/s at 131k context, 1,774 t/s cold prefill. Weights 18.2 GiB plus 0.9 GiB vision tower; 131,072 context with FP8 KV at 0.88 utilization leaves ~0.9 GiB free. Concurrency: unpatched engine crashes when spec and non-spec tokens mix; a community patch fixes it, and at 5 users MTP acceptance falls to 43 to 56% with ~25 t/s each ([cookbook](https://github.com/SergiioB/intel-arc-pro-b70-inference-cookbook/blob/master/docs/qwen38-27/QWEN38-VLLM-XPU.md), [Mann](https://jonathanmann.tech/blog/qwen38-intel-arc-b70/)). A second user on a B65 32 GB reported 40 t/s average, 17.6 t/s at 45k context with 54 s TTFT, stable over days ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/SergiioB/Qwen3.8-27B-GPTQ-Int4-sym-G128-MTP-BF16/discussions/1)). Flags used: `--quantization gptq --dtype float16 --kv-cache-dtype fp8 --enable-auto-tool-choice --tool-call-parser qwen3_xml` (or `qwen3_coder`) `--reasoning-parser qwen3 --speculative-config '{"method":"mtp","num_speculative_tokens":4}'`. Limitations: GPTQ loading regressed once in v0.19.0 ([#39474](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/39474)); the xe driver can reset a compute engine and wedge the context under sustained load (cookbook ships a watchdog); no FlashAttention, Xe2 attention is Intel's own SDPA path; TP=2 faults on B70 ([#41663](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/41663)) but that is irrelevant for one card. Prefix caching works but gave no decode speedup single-stream.

**2. llm-scaler-vllm.** Intel's fork, Docker only, B60/B70 official. Adds CCL P2P, DP scaling, ESIMD kernels, online FP8/INT4, FP8 KV, MTP for Qwen3.6, LoRA. 0.26.0-b1 (2 Sep 2026) is on vLLM 0.26 and "improves FP8 KV cache performance" and "fixes accuracy issues for Qwen models" ([Releases](https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler/releases), [README](https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler/blob/main/vllm/README.md)). Qwen3.8 is not yet in the release notes or model table (Qwen3.6-27B is, with MTP method `qwen3_5_mtp`, which is the same architecture, so it likely loads). One user filed "Arc B70 impossible to get Qwen3.5-27B running" in March (hangs, OOM, FP8 and BF16) with no visible resolution ([#339](https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler/issues/339)); Bentech later got 3.5-27B FP8 running at ~13 t/s and said "don't build vLLM from source, use Intel's image." Embedded LLM measured llm-scaler 1.2 vs vLLM 0.12 on a B60: 20 to 25% better TPOT, worse TTFT ([Embedded LLM](https://embeddedllm.com/blog/benchmarking-llm-inference-intel-arc-pro-b60)). Intel has not answered whether it replaces IPEX-LLM for single-GPU users ([#283](https://github.com/intel/llm-scaler/issues/283)).

**3. llama.cpp.** Backend choice depends on model type and quant. For dense 27B on the B70, SYCL wins after the Q8_0 reorder fix: Q8_0 went 4.88 to 15.24 t/s and now beats Q6_K (13.83), 66% of bandwidth ([PR #21527](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21527), [PMZFX findings](https://github.com/PMZFX/intel-arc-pro-b70-benchmarks/blob/master/FINDINGS.md)). Q4_K_M dense: SYCL 20.4 to 20.6 t/s, Vulkan 10.7 to 20 t/s depending on Mesa version and build ([issue #21517](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21517), [Mann](https://jonathanmann.tech/blog/intel-arc-b70-llama-cpp-benchmarks/)). For MoE, Vulkan wins after Mesa 26.1. PMZFX also found most published B70 numbers are ~50% low on prefill because Ubuntu builds lacked `-DNDEBUG`. Qwen3.8-27B specifically: SYCL MTP2 28.4 t/s, Vulkan MTP2 26.8 t/s on Q4_K_XL (Mann). Hal9000's kit lists 11 cherry-picks (Q8_0 reorder crash, missing BF16 GET_ROWS, Xe2 warptile) and rules: SYCL for dense, `-fa 0` on SYCL with MoE, `UR_L0_ENABLE_RELAXED_ALLOCATION_LIMITS=1` for >4 GB KV, `--defrag-thold 0.1` for long-lived servers ([Hal9000AIML](https://github.com/Hal9000AIML/arc-pro-b70-ubuntu-gpu-speedup-bugfixes)). Flash attention: Vulkan coopmat FA is enabled for Xe2 (KHR_coopmat), SYCL FA produced corrupt output and segfaulted with q8_0 KV on Xe2 iGPU ([#19276](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/19276)); Mann reports q4_0 KV halves decode past 16k. Vision via `--mmproj` works for Qwen3.8 (bartowski ships f16/bf16 mmproj); the cookbook runs a vision model on SYCL. Tool calling with the Qwen3 template works through llama-server's OpenAI API, which is what Hermes/Pi users run.

**4. SGLang XPU.** Exists (PyTorch 2.13+xpu, source build, `--attention-backend intel_xpu`, XPU graph, PD disaggregation, TP), verified on B580, but only MXFP4 MoE quant is documented and speculative decoding is "not yet implemented" ([SGLang XPU docs](https://lmsysorg.mintlify.app/docs/hardware-platforms/xpu)). llm-scaler-omni ships SGLang for diffusion only. Not a candidate for this workload yet.

**5. IPEX-LLM.** Archived read-only on 28 Jan 2026 with a security notice; Intel Extension for PyTorch archived the same way ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/intel-gpu-32gb-vram-local-ai-software-nvidia-keeps-winning/), [ipex-llm](https://github.com/intel/ipex-llm)). Its serving container never detected B-series GPUs ([#13324](https://github.com/intel/ipex-llm/issues/13324)). Dead end.

**6. OpenVINO.** 2026.3 (Aug 2026) adds EAGLE-3 for VLMs, prompt lookup for VLMs, stabilizes Qwen3.5/3.6 linear attention in OVMS, extends tool parsers ([Edge AI Vision](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/2026/08/intel-releases-openvino-2026-3-with-expanded-generative-ai-and-model-support/)). Intel published a B70 agent demo with OVMS running gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3-30B-A3B and Qwen3-VL-32B ([OpenVINO blog](https://medium.com/openvino-toolkit/unleashed-power-of-ai-agents-on-intel-arc-pro-b70-gpu-with-openvino-model-server-7c7e33669d1a)) and a day-0 Qwen3.8-27B post. The official `OpenVINO/Qwen3.8-27B-int4-ov` (INT4_ASYM, group 128, ratio 1.0, no AWQ) requires OpenVINO 2026.4 nightly and GenAI nightly, and its default branch fails to load without the 2026.3.1 branch ([HF](https://huggingface.co/OpenVINO/Qwen3.8-27B-int4-ov)). No B70 tok/s numbers for a 27B dense were found. Quality: plain INT4 asym RTN without AWQ/scale estimation is a weaker recipe than GPTQ/AutoRound; no published accuracy comparison. Users hit `CL_OUT_OF_RESOURCES` on Qwen3.6 with 2026.2 ([#36187](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/issues/36187)).

**7. Ollama.** Upstream now enables Vulkan by default, so Arc works generically, but there is no Intel-specific runner; the oneAPI runner is missing on Ubuntu 26.04 ([ollama #15827](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/15827)), no MTP, and speeds trail plain llama.cpp. Community images (`lexiismadd/ollama-vulkan-arc`, `eleiton/ollama-intel-arc`) fill the gap. Not worth it when llama-server exposes the same API.

## Recommendation

1. **Upstream vLLM XPU (`vllm/vllm-openai-xpu`, pinned digest) with GPTQ-INT4 G128 + BF16 MTP head, FP8 KV, 128k context.** This is the only stack with measured Qwen3.8-27B numbers on one B70 (83.7 t/s single-stream, 56 t/s at full 131k context, 1.7k t/s prefill), vision loads, and `qwen3_coder`/`qwen3` parsers work out of the box. The trade-off against your first priority: 4-bit weights, though Quesma shows Q4-class parity with BF16 on GPQA, IFBench and Terminal-Bench for this exact model, and the GDN projections and MTP tensors are kept high-precision. Costs: two or three community patches to enable BF16 draft and mixed spec batching, and a watchdog for Level-Zero resets. Run the cookbook recipe as-is rather than assembling it yourself.

2. **llama.cpp SYCL with Q8_0 (or Q6_K) GGUF + `--mmproj` + `--spec-type draft-mtp`.** Choose this if 8-bit weights outrank speed. Q8_0 is 29 GB, so on 32 GB you get maybe 16k to 24k of f16 KV; Q6_K (~22 GB) leaves room for ~64k. Expect ~15 t/s (Q8_0) to ~28 t/s (Q4 with MTP2). Build from master with `-DNDEBUG`, use f16 KV, avoid `-fa` on SYCL until #19276-class bugs are confirmed fixed on your build, and keep a Vulkan build (Mesa 26.1+) as the fallback since it is the more robust path and matches SYCL within 10% on dense decode.

3. **llm-scaler-vllm 0.26.0-b1.** The Intel-supported route and the one to watch: once Qwen3.8 and a native GPTQ+MTP path land in its release notes it becomes option 1 without patches. Today it lags upstream on Qwen3.8 and its FP8 online quant is slow on Xe2.

4. **OpenVINO Model Server.** Wait for 2026.4 stable; INT4 RTN recipe and nightly-only Qwen3.8 support make it a worse quality bet than GPTQ or Q8_0 right now.

5. SGLang, Ollama, IPEX-LLM: not viable for this model on this card in September 2026.

Precision-first alternative worth testing yourself: run the official `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B-FP8` under upstream vLLM XPU or llm-scaler (weight-only FP8, ~28 GB, ~13 t/s class based on 3.5-27B reports). It preserves 8-bit weights inside vLLM's API, but oneDNN's sporadic FP8 matmul errors on B-series and the missing scaling kernel mean you should validate outputs before trusting it for agentic coding.
